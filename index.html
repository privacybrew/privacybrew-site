The bigger picture

Data has become one of the defining currencies of the AI era — and the phone in your pocket is one of its richest sources.

Most of it is gathered quietly. Every recommendation engine, ad platform, and AI model runs on data — and it usually only surfaces where regulators require it: the "Data safety" label buried in a Play Store listing, easy to scroll past during an install.

But not every access is a red flag. A maps app needs your location — it can't route you anywhere without it. A video-calling app needs your camera and microphone — that's the entire feature. The real question isn't whether an app asks for access, it's whether that access matches what the app is actually for.

Expected
Maps → Location

Can't get you from A to B without knowing where "A" is.

Video calling → Camera & microphone

That's not extra access — that's the whole feature.

Worth a second look
Messaging app → Local network access

Sometimes it's for finding nearby devices — it can also be used to fingerprint what else is on your network across apps.

News app → Camera access

Fine if it scans documents or codes. If it doesn't do either, that access may be broader than you'd expect — worth a closer look.

PrivacyBrew doesn't tell you an app is guilty — it tells you what's unusual for what an app does, and lets you decide. The goal is awareness, not alarm. See how it works ↓

How it works

Three steps, no setup.

01 — SCAN

Read what Android already knows

PrivacyBrew asks Android's PackageManager for the apps installed on your device and their already-granted permissions — plain system metadata, not access to what's inside those apps.

02 — UNDERSTAND

Translated, not just listed

Raw permission strings become plain sentences: what an app can access, whether that's typical for what it does, and how many trackers were detected inside it.

03 — ACT

Decide what happens next

Jump straight to the right Android Settings page to revoke access, check an app's Play Store data-safety label, or opt out of a data broker — all optional, all up to you.
