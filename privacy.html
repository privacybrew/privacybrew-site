Privacy Policy — PrivacyBrew

Effective date: July 26, 2026
App: PrivacyBrew (package com.brewlabs.privacybrew)
Developer: BrewLabs
Contact: privacybrew@protonmail.com
The short version: PrivacyBrew doesn't collect your data. It doesn't have accounts, ads, analytics, or crash-reporting SDKs, and it can't connect to the internet on its own — the app has no INTERNET permission at all. Everything it shows you is read and displayed entirely on your device, and nothing about you or your installed apps is ever sent anywhere by PrivacyBrew.

The rest of this page explains exactly what that means, permission by permission, so you don't have to take our word for it.

What PrivacyBrew does

PrivacyBrew reads information Android already exposes about the apps installed on your device — their names, permissions, and (optionally) when they were last used — and displays it back to you in plain language, entirely on your device. It also links out to a few external, informational resources (described below) that you can choose to open or ignore.

Permissions PrivacyBrew uses, and why

PrivacyBrew requests exactly two permissions. Both exist solely to power the app's core feature — showing you what's installed and what it can access — and neither is used to identify you or send information off your device.

QUERY_ALL_PACKAGES — Lets PrivacyBrew see the list of apps installed on your device and read their already-granted permissions via Android's PackageManager. This is a plain system-metadata read: it doesn't require you to approve anything at runtime, and it doesn't let PrivacyBrew open, modify, or access data inside those other apps — only their name and permission/metadata, the same information Android's own Settings app can show you.

PACKAGE_USAGE_STATS (optional, off by default) — Powers a single opt-in feature: showing when you last opened an app, so you can spot ones you've forgotten about that still hold sensitive permissions. This is a special Android permission that can't be granted through a normal in-app prompt — you have to explicitly turn it on for PrivacyBrew in system Settings, and you can turn it off again at any time. If you never grant it, every other part of the app works exactly the same; you just won't see "last used" information. This data is read on-device only and is never transmitted anywhere.

PrivacyBrew does not request INTERNET, location, camera, microphone, contacts, storage, or any other permission — there was no legitimate use for any of them, so we didn't ask.

What PrivacyBrew does not do

  • No account or sign-in. There's nothing to register for and nothing tied to your identity.
  • No analytics, telemetry, or crash reporting. PrivacyBrew doesn't bundle any third-party SDK that reports back to us or anyone else.
  • No advertising. No ad SDKs, no ad IDs used, no targeting.
  • No data storage. PrivacyBrew doesn't save a database, file, or preference of what it scans — every screen is recomputed live from Android's current system state each time you open it. The one exception is a single on-device setting remembering whether you've dismissed the welcome intro screen — that's UI state, not personal or scan data, and it never leaves your device either.
  • No network calls of our own. The app has no INTERNET permission, so it is technically incapable of sending anything anywhere on its own.

External links

A few places in the app open an external link when you explicitly tap a button — for example, a data-broker's own opt-out page, or an app's official Play Store "Data safety" listing. In every case:

  • The link only opens when you tap it; nothing is fetched automatically.
  • It opens in your browser or the Google Play Store app, not inside PrivacyBrew — that external site or app handles the request, under its own privacy policy, not ours.
  • PrivacyBrew never reads, scrapes, or stores anything from those pages; it simply hands your device an address to open, the same as any regular hyperlink.

Data retention

There is nothing to retain. PrivacyBrew keeps no logs, databases, or files of what it finds on your device, on our servers (we don't have any) or anywhere else. Uninstalling the app removes 100% of anything it ever touched.

Children's privacy

PrivacyBrew is not directed at children and does not knowingly collect any information from anyone, children included — it doesn't collect information from anyone at all.

Changes to this policy

If this policy ever changes — for example, if a future version adds a feature that changes what data is read or how — we'll update this page and the in-app version with a new effective date before that change ships.
